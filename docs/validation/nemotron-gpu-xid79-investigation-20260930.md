# RTX 3060 PCIe bus-loss investigation — 30 September 2026

The failure is confirmed at the guest NVIDIA driver/device boundary, but the initiating cause is **not established**. On VM108 (`sandbox`) at 2026-09-30 02:16:22.379 UTC, driver 610.57.04 reported Xid 79 (`GPU has fallen off the bus`), then Xid 154 requesting PF FLR. A repeated Nemotron Vulkan diarization request subsequently returned `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST` (`vkWaitForFences: -4`). PCI inventory still lists the virtual function, but `nvidia-smi` cannot reach a usable GPU. Neither an exposed reset method nor virtual PCI enumeration proves the physical card or link is healthy.

## Timeline and attribution

- The first AMI ES2004a 530–630-second Vulkan diarization request finished in 69.10 seconds; previous 350–450-second request finished in 30.93 seconds. The difference is not isolated to a cause. Both returned saved 67-span outputs before the failure.
- The repeated 530–630-second request began as a Piclaw `bash` tool call at 02:15:58.409 UTC. It ran the same binary from `ede9ea01`, model and WAV. Its 24.09-second process failed without JSON output; command, stderr and timing are in [the incident record](../../benchmarks/speech-foundations/ami-pilot-contract-20260913/nemotron-530-630-vulkan-failure.txt).
- The **first** guest kernel GPU error in the preceding 03-minute window is Xid 79 at 02:16:22.379017 UTC, then Xid 154 at 02:16:22.379067 UTC. The application log does not timestamp the precise fence return, so the available evidence does not order a shader/driver fault against the physical link becoming unreachable at subsecond resolution.
- The Vulkan tower reported `layer 29: Vulkan submission still in flight`, followed by `Vulkan submission state uncertain`, `Vulkan device lost` and `vkWaitForFences: -4`. `backends/vulkan/vulkan_lifetime.go` deliberately retains a submitted plan on unexpected fence failure and translates `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST` to process-level device loss; `backends/vulkan/vulkan_dispatch.go` waits after each successful queue submission. Layer 29 identifies where the failure was observed, **not necessarily the command that initiated it**. A previous layer or device/host failure can surface asynchronously at a later fence.
- At 02:16:22.474 UTC the NVIDIA kernel logged `nvidia_dev_put` warnings on the Vulkan process's close path. They occurred about 96 ms **after** Xid 79, so they do not establish that close caused the bus loss.

The guest is QEMU i440FX. Host `borg` maps physical `0000:01:00` GPU and audio functions into VM108 as guest `0000:00:10.x` (`hostpci0=0000:01:00`; GPU and audio share host IOMMU group 13). The physical host runs kernel 7.0.14-17-pve and Proxmox 9.2.20. The returned VM config has `vga=virtio-gl` and no explicit `machine` or `pcie=1` value. Its bounded syslog export covers 02:10:12–03:24:52 **Europe/Lisbon**, including the 03:16:22 WEST guest incident. All 663 returned entries were checked: no logged PCIe/AER/DPC, VFIO, IOMMU, NVIDIA/Xid, VM108 reset, or host restart event in that export. UPS polls at **03:16:20** and **03:16:52 WEST** (two seconds before and 30 seconds after the failure) reported online mains, 100% charge and no critical action. A clean host syslog and online UPS do not measure physical GPU rail power, card temperature, slot contacts or PCIe signal integrity at the instant of failure. Host passthrough evidence: `/workspace/exports/gpu-incident-20260930T021622Z/hypervisor-assessment.md`, `proxmox-host-initial.json`, `proxmox-host-syslog.json` and `proxmox-host-syslog-relevant.txt`; the first JSON contains a separate rejected timezone-suffixed read-only request, while the bounded syslog request completed. The corrected VM108 task query for 02:10–02:25 UTC returned no entries. A separate local host boot export (`/workspace/exports/gpu-incident-20260930T021622Z/proxmox-boot-relevant.txt`) records `intel_iommu=on`, AER/DPC control granted to the OS and firmware-declared ASPM unsupported. At VM108 startup on **19 September**, QEMU logged a 16 GiB BAR DMA-map `-22 Invalid argument` and `PCI peer-to-peer transactions on BARs are not supported`; VFIO GPU/audio resets completed. Successful GPU runs occurred later and the incident-time host export has no matching new mapping/reset error, so these boot warnings identify a passthrough constraint, **not** a demonstrated cause of the September 30 bus loss. Guest capture: `/workspace/exports/gpu-incident-20260930T021622Z/host-diagnostics.txt` (despite its name, it is guest-only).

## Why the workload alone does not explain it

The same guest GPU lost the bus at 2026-09-19 14:42:48 UTC, 2026-09-19 19:44:02 UTC and 2026-09-26 10:21:52 UTC on driver **580.173.02**, before this Nemotron Vulkan request and before driver 610.57.04. Those three incidents reported Xid 79 then Xid 154 (`Node Reboot Required`), whereas the current driver reports PF FLR. The September 19 cases occurred during Qwen evaluation; September 26 occurred during CUDA sanitizer/MoJev testing. Earlier guest Xid 31 MMU faults were also logged on September 22, but **not** in the September 30 pre-fault window. Previous software audits identified and fixed a CUDA thread-context boundary defect, yet no causal link to the bus losses was demonstrated. The recurrence across workloads, APIs and two driver branches weakens a theory specific to one Vulkan shader or one driver version. It does not exonerate application workloads, a shared runtime/device use pattern, the physical card, power, PCIe link, firmware, or passthrough.

The current guest shows `00:10.0` bound to `nvidia`, virtual `D0`, `reset_method=flr`, and reported 16.0 GT/s ×16. Those are read-only **virtual PCI** observations taken after the event, not measurements of the physical host link during it. There is no continuous power/temperature/clock trace, physical AER/link-state counter capture, or completed NVIDIA crash dump for this incident. No root cause can be assigned from Xid 79/154 or `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST` alone.

## Discriminating checks and safety gate

1. Preserve the existing guest kernel journal, Piclaw execution timestamps, VM/host syslog exports and any GPU crash state. The NVIDIA driver said a crash dump was created, but a complete dump has not been recovered. A full `nvidia-bug-report.sh` can hang or query the failed GPU; coordinate a bounded capture with the host owner before attempting it. Do not unload the driver merely to collect diagnostics.
2. Obtain **physical-host** (not guest-emulated) GPU and upstream-port PCI config/link status, AER/DPC counters, VFIO/QEMU logs and any board/PSU power evidence around the fault. The Proxmox API inventory and syslog presently do not supply instant link counters or GPU rail readings. Absence of syslog AER is not proof that no link or power transient occurred.
3. Only after Rui authorises recovery, follow the host owner's staged plan: recover the device, verify both functions and baseline health, and capture continuous physical-host/guest PCIe, GPU temperature/power/clock and journal telemetry before any small isolated workload. Run ordinary and instrumented tests in separate processes with explicit fences, stop on the first driver/query error, and do not blindly rerun the full 100-second Vulkan request. A reboot/reset by itself is not a fix.
4. If physical-link or rail errors recur under independent small GPU workloads, investigate card, slot, power wiring/PSU and passthrough platform first. If a reproducible error is isolated to one shader/driver path while physical telemetry remains stable, pursue instrumented code/driver attribution. These are conditional tests, not present findings.

No GPU workload, reset, driver reload, service restart or reboot was performed for this investigation. The [MoJev bus-loss audit](mojev-gpu-bus-loss-audit-20260926.md) and [Qwen crash audit](qwen3-gpu-crash-audit-20260919.md) contain earlier software reviews and their limitations. GPU testing remains stopped pending explicit operator approval and host-owner coordination.
